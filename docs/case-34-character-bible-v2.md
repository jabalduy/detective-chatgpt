# Character bible v2 — memorable passengers and tonal rules

Status: **proposed, not approved final canon**. This document revises the working story concept in `case-34-narrative-blueprint.md`. The original English-name cast is superseded for discussion purposes only. No game JSON, code, or frontend changes are authorized by this draft.

## Tone

Classic serious 1928 luxury-train mystery with witty dialogue, distinct personalities, social awkwardness, romance, and occasional comic relief. The murder matters and is not a punchline. Avoid graphic medical descriptions, gore, and dwelling on suffering. Physical evidence may include restrained mentions of blood, stained fabric, injury, or an object used in the crime. Humor comes from character, not from mocking the victim's death.

**Naming rule:** short names, recognizable speech patterns and silhouettes; passengers can have period-appropriate surnames in their dossier, but spoken dialogue favors first names and memorable titles.

## Victim (not a suspect)

**Viktor** — affluent businessman and manipulator. Expects special treatment, publicly humiliates others, and privately keeps compromising papers. His death is serious, but his behavior supplies many credible motives. In the opening reception he makes a pointed comment about the sealed portfolio; several passengers react.

## Eight principal suspects

| Name / role | Instant recognition | Public behavior | Private secret / investigative purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Klara**, business partner | Dark tailored suit, gloves, immaculate posture | Precise, unfailingly courteous, never raises her voice | **Fixed killer** in current working premise. Hid fraudulent relief-fund records and staged the crime. Her composure occasionally cracks over ledger details. |
| **Maks**, nephew | Bright waistcoat, flashy pocket watch | Boastful, impulsive, treats everything as a competition | Stole a private letter and is frightened of losing inheritance; a real lie, but not murder. |
| **Dr. Roza**, physician | Small medical case, severe glasses | Dry sarcasm, speaks in clipped diagnostic statements | Conceals an earlier professional mistake; can distinguish the victim's medication from the cause and likely timing of death without gruesome exposition. |
| **Boris**, security chief | Tall, broad-shouldered, meticulously polished boots | Few words, intimidatingly formal | Secretly involved with **Sofi**. His false alibi protects their relationship, not the killer. Notices who used the service corridor. |
| **Marta**, senior attendant | Keys and a notebook, always in motion | Chatty, knows everyone's preferences and rumors | Helped move a covered trolley without realizing what it carried; fears losing her job. |
| **Sofi**, singer | Dramatic scarf and evening gloves | Theatrical, expressive, talks in sweeping metaphors | Secret romance with **Boris**; heard the earlier dining-salon confrontation. |
| **Leo**, journalist | Notebook, rumpled coat | Playful, nosy, quick with jokes | Investigating financial fraud; has a copy of a document someone else may destroy. His professional agenda makes him selective about what he reveals. |
| **Graf** (Count Anton), aristocrat | Monocle, perfect handkerchief, tea ritual | Fussy, old-fashioned, complains about etiquette | Has surprisingly precise observations of clothing, timing and service habits. Hides a family debt to Viktor and initially omits it. |

The eight suspects above replace the earlier eight-person working list; **Viktor is the ninth major character as the victim**, not an extra suspect. A conductor, porter, and minor staff may appear as supporting witnesses without expanding the suspect roster.

## Character-voice examples (draft, not final dialogue)

- **Graf:** «Убийство в первом классе? Я намерен написать жалобу. Хотя сперва, пожалуй, стоит найти убийцу».
- **Roza:** «Ваше предположение весьма смелое. Жаль, что факты о нём не осведомлены».
- **Maks:** «Я бы раскрыл это дело за полчаса. Если бы мне не мешали».
- **Marta:** «Я ничего не подслушивала. Просто у дверей здесь удивительно хорошая слышимость».
- **Boris:** «Я был на посту». [Пауза.] «Почти всё время».
- **Sofi:** «Я не устраиваю сцен. Я живу на сцене. Это совершенно разные вещи».
- **Leo:** «Я обещаю ничего не печатать. До завтрашнего выпуска».
- **Klara:** «Давайте отделим впечатления от фактов. Иначе мы обвиним первого, кто нам не понравился».

Speech patterns should survive without speaker labels; do not turn every exchange into a one-liner. Serious reveals are allowed to breathe.

## Hidden relationship: Boris and Sofi

They appear incompatible: imposing, taciturn Boris and flamboyant, talkative Sofi. In public they spar and act annoyed by each other. In private they have a tender, sincere romance. Each gives an evasive answer about a short absence on the night of the murder because they were together. Players may discover the relationship through a harmless affectionate note, matching recollections, or an accidental verbal slip.

**Fair-play consequence:** exposing the romance resolves their alibi inconsistency and opens a witness route, but is **not required** to solve the murder. The relationship is not a joke about romance itself; the humor is their conspicuously unsuccessful attempt to conceal it.

## Interlocking secrets and misdirection

1. **Maks's stolen letter** appears to implicate him in Viktor's death, but it relates to inheritance and a different financial conflict. Leo has context for the document.
2. **Marta's trolley movement** connects the dining salon to the compartment. She initially hides it to protect her employment; Boris's corridor observations and service logs can independently corroborate the route.
3. **Boris and Sofi's alibis** seem mutually inconsistent until their romance is uncovered; Sofi's account of the earlier argument still matters.
4. **Graf's etiquette obsession** yields a concrete clue: a passenger's gloves or cufflinks changed between reception and later appearance. That observation is useful only when cross-checked with another trace; it is not a magical instant identification.
5. **Roza's withheld mistake** makes her initially evasive about medication, but she can still provide a bounded, credible medical opinion on the misleading time of death.
6. **Klara's politeness** makes her seem reliable while she steers conversations toward the tunnel. Her financial motive and physical connection must be provable through at least two routes; do not rely on a single incriminating confession.

## Investigation design requirements

- Give every suspect one distinctive opening encounter, one truthful helpful fact, one meaningful concealment, and at least one change in demeanor after a reveal.
- Ensure at least three independent paths to the **earlier murder / staged compartment** revelation, and two corroborated paths to **Klara's guilt**.
- Treat comedic side plots as optional investigative rewards, not mandatory bottlenecks.
- Keep 3–4 urgent decisions with 10-second deadlines and unconditional defaults. Losing a letter, linen trace, or witness opportunity must not block victory.
- Preserve the fixed 60-minute investigation + 5-minute accusation rules, limited questions, escalating-cost hints, and operator-only interface.
- Avoid anachronistic jokes, modern slang in 1928 dialogue, and heavy-handed stereotypes.
- The body and forensic clues can be described in one or two neutral sentences; no graphic imagery or elaborate violence.
